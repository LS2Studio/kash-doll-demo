The story

420 Million Spotify Streams

Kash Doll is Detroit's loudest female voice. Ten years deep, three RIAA Gold singles, four seasons on Starz, and she is just getting started.

Born Arkeisha Antoinette Knight on March 14, 1989, the oldest of six siblings, Kash Doll grew up on Joy Road in Detroit and started working young to help her single mother support the family. She studied business management at Henry Ford College for a year, then worked as an exotic dancer to keep the family afloat. One night she made $26,000. She used the money to invest in two brick-and-mortar beauty bars and studio time, then quit dancing to bet on herself.

Her first Detroit gigs came in 2014. She sold mixtapes out of her trunk and built her following one freestyle at a time on Instagram. Drake used Instagram to invite her to open his Summer Sixteen tour stop in Detroit. She remixed Tinashe's "2 On" in 2015, and her "Run Me My Money" video went viral the same year. "For Everybody" broke out in 2017. "So Good" with Big Sean and Metro Boomin followed, her most streamed song at 113 million Spotify streams. She signed to Republic Records in 2018, and her debut album Stacked entered the Billboard 200 at No. 76 in 2019, the same year she earned a BET Award nomination.

After a first record deal she called "the worst thing I've ever done," she built her own table. Kash Doll Enterprises, her own company, is the label behind The Last Doll and Back on Dexter, released under exclusive license to MNRK Records. She has always been clear-eyed about money. In 2019 she bought a seven-bedroom waterfront mansion in Grosse Pointe for $1.935 million. She sold it in May 2022 for $1.755 million, taking a loss of roughly $180,000, and she does not regret it. "It was too much. And I got pregnant. I wanted to be in Atlanta. That was a lot to keep up. I wanted my family there. It just was too much. I just needed what would have me at peace."

She is a mother of two: her son Kashton, born January 6, 2022, and her daughter, born June 12, 2024. Motherhood changed the music. "I'm The Prize," released November 6, 2026 with Ebony Riley, is her most personal single yet, a song about power, money and emotional manipulation in a relationship. She pictured Method Man in the video from the start. "When I was making it, I seen Method Man the whole time. I have to have him." He learned the lyrics in one day and arrived on set ready to perform. The video, directed by WhoIsHalo and produced by OctaneThisThatGas, runs 3:24, longer than the 2:49 single, and tells a full story.

"If you in a relationship that both of you guys are watering each other, I feel like both of you guys are the prize. Nobody's the prize when one person is drained and the other person is walking around the field."

Kash Doll, PEOPLE, Nov 10, 2026

The giving back runs through Detroit too. Her nonprofit, the Fairy Boss Muva Foundation, runs an annual Turkey and Produce Giveaway, now in its 12th year. The most recent ran November 21, 2026 at DTLR Belmont Plaza, 2000 E 8 Mile Rd, in partnership with DTLR and 97.9 WJLB. It started because her mother could not afford a prom dress for her, and she decided no girl in her city should feel that. In 2019 she earned the Spirit of Detroit Award for her charitable work through Kash Doll Enterprises, and was named Best Local Rapper in the 313 Best in Black Detroit awards.

"Detroit is tough, and I'm about that life."

Kash Doll, NYLON

Ten years in the game, and Kash Doll closed the doll era on her own terms. The Last Doll is her most personal project: songs about her kids, self love and growth, next to the unapologetic bars that built her name. Released on her own Kash Doll Enterprises with MNRK Records.

Beyond music

Boss Moves, Detroit Roots

She studied business management before she ever signed a record deal. The company, the beauty bars and the giving back all run through Detroit.

Her company

Kash Doll Enterprises, Inc.

Her own company, and the label behind The Last Doll and Back on Dexter, released under exclusive license to MNRK Records. After a first record deal she called "the worst thing I've ever done," she built her own table. The company also runs her charitable work, which earned her the Spirit of Detroit Award in 2019.

Founded and run by Kash Doll  •  Label for The Last Doll (2024) and Back on Dexter (2023)

First investment

The Beauty Bars

Before the record deal, there was the business plan. Kash Doll took the money she earned dancing, including a $26,000 night, and invested it in two brick-and-mortar beauty bars and studio time, all while still in her twenties. The beauty bars funded the music. The music funded everything after it.

Funded from her dancing earnings  •  Her first businesses, before Republic Records

Community

B.A.D. Girls Initiative

Black American Doll Girls. The initiative donates dresses and styles young women in the Detroit area during prom season, and sponsors community events across the city. It is the same mission as the foundation, aimed at the girls who are where she once was.
